Course project · AST5220 Cosmology II · University of Oslo · July 31, 2026

Computing the CMB power spectrum

Iván Villegas-Pérez — Astronomy & Astrophysics manuscript style

Read PDF ↗ GitHub ↗

Context. The goal of this whole study is to be able to predict the CMB (and matter) fluctuations (described by the so-called power-spectrum) from first principles and learn about all the different physical processes that goes on to be able to explain the results.

Aims. The goals in this work are to make a class/module that takes in the cosmological parameters and has functions for getting the Hubble parameter, conformal time and distance measures as function of scale factor and the variable $x = \ln a$, to make a class/module that takes in the abundance of Helium in the Universe and has functions for getting the free electron fraction (including both hydrogen and Helium recombination, and hydrogen and Helium reionization), the optical depth (and its derivatives) and the visibility function (and its derivatives), to make a class/module that evolves the density, velocity, temperature, polarization and neutrino perturbations from an early time until today, and to make a class/module that computes the CMB power-spectrum including the contributions from massless neutrinos and photon polarization.

Methods. Four codes have been developed, using the template given by Hans A. Winther. In the first code, several functions have been implemented to solve the background cosmology of the Universe and plot the results, identifying each epoch domination. In the second code, several functions have been implemented to solve the recombination history of the Universe, now including a non-zero Helium fraction and a hydrogen/Helium reionization model. In the third one, several functions have been implemented to evolve the density, velocity, potential, photon temperature and polarization, and neutrino perturbations. Lastly, in the fourth code, several functions have been implemented to compute the CMB power-spectrum, including the neutrino and polarization source terms.

Results. Background cosmology of the Universe has been solved and each epoch has been determined. The age of the Universe has also been computed, being this $t_{\rm Universe} \approx 13.86$ Gyr. Recombination history of the Universe has been solved, now including Helium and reionization. The sound horizon at decoupling has been computed, being this $r_s \approx 112.309$ Mpc. Evolution of the structure of the Universe has been solved, including neutrino and photon polarization perturbations. The CMB power-spectrum has been computed with the full physics (neutrinos, Helium, reionization and polarization), along with a CMB map.

Conclusions. The CMB power-spectrum has been computed with a high accuracy, now including neutrinos, Helium, reionization and photon polarization, considering the approximations made.

Key words. background cosmology – evolution of the Universe – epoch domination – recombination – structure evolution – perturbations – CMB power-spectrum – CMB map

1. Introduction

The goals of this study have been to solve the evolution of the uniform background cosmology in the Universe, to solve its recombination history, to solve the evolution of structure in the Universe and to compute the CMB power-spectrum [1].

For this first goal, a class/module has been implemented. This class takes the given cosmological parameters and (with the use of different functions) returns the Hubble parameter ($H$), conformal time ($\eta$) and distance measures as function of the scale factor ($a$) and the variable $x$ ($x = \ln a$), which will be the main time variable for this work [2]. For the second goal, a different class/module has been implemented to compute the optical depth ($\tau$) as a function of $x$ and its derivatives, and the visibility function $\tilde{g}$ and its derivatives [3]. For the third goal, a class/module to construct two-dimensional functions (of $x$ and $k$) for each of the main physical quantities of interest: $\Phi(x,k)$, $\Psi(x,k)$, $\delta_{\rm CDM}(x,k)$, $\delta_b(x,k)$, $v_{\rm CDM}(x,k)$, $v_B(x,k)$ and $\Theta_\ell(x,k)$ [4]. Finally, the CMB and matter power spectrum has been computed [5].

This work extends an earlier, simplified version of the code (which neglected neutrinos, Helium, reionization and photon polarization) by including all four of these ingredients, together with several bug fixes. The most significant were: a sign error in the Helium-reionization redshift ($z = e^{-x}-1$ instead of $e^x-1$), which kept the Helium reionization term artificially switched on for most of the dark ages and prevented the acoustic peaks from appearing at the correct location; a normalization bug where the reionization contribution to $X_e$ was being fed back into, and accumulated by, the Peebles ODE instead of being evaluated once per grid point; a missing $\sqrt{(\ell+2)!/(\ell-2)!}$ prefactor in the polarization source term, which left the EE and TE spectra systematically too small; and a spurious $|\cdot|$ in the line-of-sight $C_\ell$ integral that suppressed the physical sign changes of the TE cross-spectrum.

The fiducial cosmology is the best-fit from Planck 2018 [6], now including neutrinos, Helium, reionization and photon polarization: $h_0 = 0.67$, $T_{\rm CMB,0} = 2.7255\,\text{K}$, $N_{\rm eff} = 3.046$, $\Omega_{B,0} = 0.05$, $\Omega_{\rm CDM,0} = 0.267$, $\Omega_{k,0} = 0$, $Y_p = 0.245$, $z_{\rm reion} = 8$, $\Delta z_{\rm reion} = 0.5$, $z_{\rm He,reion} = 3.5$, $\Delta z_{\rm He,reion} = 0.5$, $n_s = 0.965$, $A_s = 2.1\cdot10^{-9}$.

2. Basic cosmology

2.1. Background Cosmology

Let’s review the theory basics. The goal of this work has been computing the expansion history of the universe, and look at the uniform background densities of the various matter and energy components. Let’s first define the Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker metric (here for a flat space where $k = 0$),

$$ds^2 = -c^2\,dt^2 + a^2(t)\!\left[dr^2 + r^2\!\left(d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2\right)\right],$$
(1)

or in Cartesian coordinates $ds^2 = a^2(t)(d\eta^2+dx^2+dy^2+dz^2)$, where $a(t)$ is the scale factor and $\eta$ is the conformal time. We use $x \equiv \ln a$ as the main time variable, and the redshift $1+z = a_0/a(t)$. The Friedmann equation reads

$$H = H_0\sqrt{\Omega_{M,0}\,a^{-3}+\Omega_{R,0}\,a^{-4}+\Omega_{k,0}\,a^{-2}+\Omega_{\Lambda,0}},$$
(4)

where $H \equiv \dot{a}/a$, $\Omega_{M,0} = \Omega_{B,0}+\Omega_{\rm CDM,0}$ and $\Omega_{R,0} = \Omega_{\nu,0}+\Omega_{\gamma,0}$. The scaled Hubble parameter is $\mathcal{H} \equiv aH$. Each component evolves as

$$\rho_{\rm CDM} = \rho_{\rm CDM,0}\,a^{-3},\quad \rho_B = \rho_{B,0}\,a^{-3},\quad \rho_\gamma = \rho_{\gamma,0}\,a^{-4},\quad \rho_\nu = \rho_{\nu,0}\,a^{-4},\quad \rho_\Lambda = \rho_{\Lambda,0}.$$
(6–10)

The radiation densities follow from the observed CMB temperature:

$$\Omega_{\gamma,0} = 2\cdot\frac{\pi^2}{30}\frac{(k_BT_{\rm CMB,0})^4}{\hbar^3c^5}\cdot\frac{8\pi G}{3H_0^2},\qquad \Omega_{\nu,0} = N_{\rm eff}\cdot\left(\frac{7}{8}\cdot\frac{4}{11}\right)^{4/3}\Omega_{\gamma,0}.$$
(17–18)

The conformal time satisfies $d\eta/dx = c/\mathcal{H}$, solved with initial condition $\eta(x_{\rm start}) = c/\mathcal{H}(x_{\rm start})$.

$$\frac{d\eta}{dx} = \frac{c}{\mathcal{H}}.$$
(22)

For a flat Universe all standard distance measures follow: $r = \eta_0-\eta$, $d_A = ar$, $d_L = r/a^2$. The cosmic time follows from $dt/dx = 1/H(x)$, with initial condition $t(x_{\rm start}) = 1/(2H(x_{\rm start}))$.

2.2. Recombination History

The optical depth is

$$\tau(\eta) = \int_\eta^{\eta_0}n_e\sigma_T a\,d\eta',\qquad \tau' = \frac{d\tau}{dx} = -\frac{cn_e\sigma_T}{\mathcal{H}}.$$
(32–33)

We focus on the fractional electron density $X_e \equiv n_e/n_H$. Including Helium, only a fraction $1-Y_p$ of the baryons are hydrogen:

$$n_H = (1-Y_p)\,\frac{\Omega_{B,0}\rho_{c,0}}{m_H a^3}.$$
(35)

The Saha equation (used when $X_e > 0.99$):

$$\frac{X_e^2}{1-X_e} = \frac{1}{n_B}\left(\frac{m_eT_B}{2\pi}\right)^{3/2}e^{-\epsilon_0/T_B},$$
(36)

where $\epsilon_0 = 13.6\,\text{eV}$. The Peebles’ equation (used when $X_e < 0.99$):

$$\frac{dX_e}{dx} = \frac{C_r(T_b)}{H}\!\left[\beta(T_b)(1-X_e)-n_H\alpha^{(2)}(T_b)X_e^2\right],$$
(37)

where the various coefficients $C_r$, $\Lambda_{2s\to1s}$, $\Lambda_\alpha$, $\beta$, $\alpha^{(2)}$, $\phi_2$ are given by Equations (38)–(46) in the report.

2.2.1. Reionization

A phenomenological tanh reionization model is added on top of the first recombination solution. For hydrogen (and simultaneous first Helium) reionization:

$$X_e^{H,\rm reion}(x) = \frac{f_H}{2}\!\left[1+\tanh\!\left(\frac{y_{\rm reion}-y}{\Delta y_{\rm reion}}\right)\right],$$
(47)

where $f_H = 1+Y_p/[4(1-Y_p)]$, $y=(1+z)^{3/2}$, $y_{\rm reion}=(1+z_{\rm reion})^{3/2}$, $\Delta y_{\rm reion} = \frac{3}{2}(1+z_{\rm reion})^{1/2}\Delta z_{\rm reion}$. For the second Helium reionization:

$$X_e^{He,\rm reion}(x) = \frac{f_{He}}{2}\!\left[1+\tanh\!\left(\frac{z_{He,\rm reion}-z}{\Delta z_{He,\rm reion}}\right)\right],\qquad f_{He} = \frac{Y_p}{4(1-Y_p)}.$$
(48)

The total free electron fraction is

$$X_e(x) = X_e^{\rm Saha/Peebles}(x)+X_e^{H,\rm reion}(x)+X_e^{He,\rm reion}(x).$$
(49)

The visibility function $\tilde{g}(x) = -\tau'(x)e^{-\tau(x)}$ satisfies $\int_{-\infty}^0\tilde{g}(x)dx=1$ and is sharply peaked at $x\approx-7$ ($z\sim1100$). The sound horizon at decoupling:

$$\frac{ds}{dx} = \frac{c_s}{\mathcal{H}},\qquad c_s = c\sqrt{\frac{R}{3(1+R)}},\qquad R = \frac{4\Omega_{\gamma,0}}{3\Omega_{B,0}a}.$$
(51)

2.3. Perturbations

The linearized Einstein and Boltzmann equations for photons, baryons, dark matter and neutrinos are solved numerically. At early times ($\tau$ very large) the tight-coupling approximation is used; at later times the line-of-sight integration approach of Zaldarriaga and Seljak [10] is used, requiring only $\ell_{\rm max}=8$ multipoles in the perturbation stage.

2.3.1. The full system

Photon temperature multipoles ($\Pi = \Theta_2+\Theta_0^P+\Theta_2^P$, $R = 4\Omega_{\gamma,0}/(3\Omega_{B,0}a)$):

$$\Theta_0' = -\frac{ck}{\mathcal{H}}\Theta_1-\Phi',$$
(52)
$$\Theta_1' = \frac{ck}{3\mathcal{H}}\Theta_0-\frac{2ck}{3\mathcal{H}}\Theta_2+\frac{ck}{3\mathcal{H}}\Psi+\tau'\!\left[\Theta_1+\frac{1}{3}v_b\right],$$
(53)
$$\Theta_\ell' = \frac{\ell ck}{(2\ell+1)\mathcal{H}}\Theta_{\ell-1}-\frac{(\ell+1)ck}{(2\ell+1)\mathcal{H}}\Theta_{\ell+1}+\tau'\!\left[\Theta_\ell-\frac{\Pi\delta_{\ell,2}}{10}\right],\quad 2\le\ell<\ell_{\rm max}.$$
(54)

Photon polarization multipoles:

$$\Theta_0^{P\prime} = -\frac{ck}{\mathcal{H}}\Theta_1^P+\tau'\!\left[\Theta_0^P-\frac{1}{2}\Pi\right],$$
(56)
$$\Theta_\ell^{P\prime} = \frac{\ell ck}{(2\ell+1)\mathcal{H}}\Theta_{\ell-1}^P-\frac{(\ell+1)ck}{(2\ell+1)\mathcal{H}}\Theta_{\ell+1}^P+\tau'\!\left[\Theta_\ell^P-\frac{\Pi\delta_{\ell,2}}{10}\right],\quad 1\le\ell<\ell_{\rm max}.$$
(57)

Neutrino multipoles:

$$N_0' = -\frac{ck}{\mathcal{H}}N_1-\Phi',\qquad N_1' = \frac{ck}{3\mathcal{H}}N_0-\frac{2ck}{3\mathcal{H}}N_2+\frac{ck}{3\mathcal{H}}\Psi,$$
(59–60)
$$N_\ell' = \frac{\ell ck}{(2\ell+1)\mathcal{H}}N_{\ell-1}-\frac{(\ell+1)ck}{(2\ell+1)\mathcal{H}}N_{\ell+1},\quad 2\le\ell<\ell_{\rm max,\nu}.$$
(61)

Cold dark matter and baryons:

$$\delta_{\rm CDM}' = \frac{ck}{\mathcal{H}}v_{\rm CDM}-3\Phi',\qquad v_{\rm CDM}' = -v_{\rm CDM}-\frac{ck}{\mathcal{H}}\Psi,$$
(63–64)
$$\delta_B' = \frac{ck}{\mathcal{H}}v_B-3\Phi',\qquad v_B' = -v_B-\frac{ck}{\mathcal{H}}\Psi+\tau'R(3\Theta_1+v_B).$$
(65–66)

Metric perturbations:

$$\Phi' = \frac{H_0^2}{2\mathcal{H}^2}\!\left[\frac{\Omega_{\rm CDM,0}}{a}\delta_{\rm CDM}+\frac{\Omega_{B,0}}{a}\delta_B+4\frac{\Omega_{\gamma,0}}{a^2}\Theta_0+4\frac{\Omega_{\nu,0}}{a^2}N_0\right]+\Psi-\frac{c^2k^2}{3\mathcal{H}^2}\Phi,$$
(67)
$$\Psi = -\Phi-\frac{12H_0^2}{c^2k^2a^2}\!\left[\Omega_{\gamma,0}\Theta_2+\Omega_{\nu,0}N_2\right].$$
(68)

2.3.2. The tight coupling regime

In tight coupling only $\Theta_0$ and $\Theta_1$ are evolved. The expressions for $\Theta_1'$ and $v_B'$ become:

$$q = -\frac{\text{Num.}}{(1+R)\tau'+\mathcal{H}'/\mathcal{H}-1},$$
(71)
$$v_B' = \frac{1}{1+R}\!\left[-v_B-\frac{ck}{\mathcal{H}}\Psi+R\!\left(q+\frac{ck}{\mathcal{H}}(-\Theta_0+2\Theta_2)-\frac{ck}{\mathcal{H}}\Psi\right)\right],\qquad\Theta_1' = \frac{1}{3}(q-v_B').$$
(72–73)

Higher-order moments in tight coupling: $\Theta_2 = -8ck/(15\mathcal{H}\tau')\,\Theta_1$ (with polarization), $\Theta_\ell = -[\ell/(2\ell+1)]\,[ck/(\mathcal{H}\tau')]\,\Theta_{\ell-1}$ for $\ell>2$, $\Theta_0^P = \frac{5}{4}\Theta_2$, $\Theta_1^P = -[ck/(4\mathcal{H}\tau')]\Theta_2$, $\Theta_2^P = \frac{1}{4}\Theta_2$.

2.3.3. Initial conditions

Deep in the tight-coupling regime, with $f_\nu = \Omega_{\nu,0}/(\Omega_{\gamma,0}+\Omega_{\nu,0})$:

$$\Psi = -\frac{1}{\tfrac{2}{3}+\tfrac{2}{5}f_\nu},\quad\Phi = -\!\left(1+\frac{2f_\nu}{5}\right)\Psi,\quad\delta_{\rm CDM}=\delta_B=-\frac{3}{2}\Psi,\quad v_{\rm CDM}=v_B=-\frac{ck}{2\mathcal{H}}\Psi,$$
(80–83)
$$\Theta_0=-\frac{1}{2}\Psi,\quad\Theta_1=\frac{ck}{6\mathcal{H}}\Psi,\quad N_0=-\frac{1}{2}\Psi,\quad N_1=\frac{ck}{6\mathcal{H}}\Psi,\quad N_2=-\frac{c^2k^2a^2(\Phi+\Psi)}{12H_0^2\Omega_{\nu,0}}.$$
(84–85, 92–94)

2.4. CMB power-spectrum

The CMB power spectrum is $C_\ell \equiv \langle|a_{\ell m}|^2\rangle$. Using the line-of-sight integration method [10]:

$$\Theta_\ell(k,x=0) = \int_{-\infty}^0\tilde{S}(k,x)\,j_\ell\!\left[k(\eta_0-\eta)\right]dx,$$
(98)

where the source function is

$$\tilde{S}(k,x) = \tilde{g}\!\left[\Theta_0+\Psi+\frac{1}{4}\Pi\right]+e^{-\tau}(\Psi'-\Phi')-\frac{1}{ck}\frac{d}{dx}(\mathcal{H}\tilde{g}v_b)+\frac{3}{4c^2k^2}\frac{d}{dx}\!\left[\mathcal{H}\frac{d}{dx}(\mathcal{H}\tilde{g}\Pi)\right].$$
(99)

The four terms correspond to: (1) the monopole/Sachs-Wolfe term, (2) the Integrated Sachs-Wolfe effect (changing gravitational potentials), (3) a Doppler term, and (4) a polarization/quadrupole correction. The CMB power spectrum then reads

$$C_\ell = 4\pi\int_0^\infty A_s\!\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm pivot}}\right)^{n_s-1}\Theta_\ell^2(k)\,\frac{dk}{k}.$$
(102)

The matter power-spectrum:

$$P(k,x) = |\Delta_M(k,x)|^2\,P_{\rm prim}(k),\qquad\Delta_M(k,x)\equiv\frac{c^2k^2\Phi(k,x)}{\tfrac{3}{2}\Omega_{M,0}a^{-1}H_0^2}.$$
(103)

A CMB map is generated from the theoretical $C_\ell$ spectrum using HEALPix [11] and Healpy [13], drawing $a_{\ell m} = \sqrt{-\ln(A)C_\ell}\,e^{2\pi i\theta}$ with $a_{\ell,-m}=(-1)^ma_{\ell m}^*$.

3. Implementation method

Four C++ classes have been implemented (all code is publicly available on GitHub): BackgroundCosmology, RecombinationHistory, Perturbations and PowerSpectrum.

The BackgroundCosmology class computes $\Omega_{\gamma,0}$, $\Omega_{\nu,0}$, $\Omega_{\Lambda,0}$ and provides splined functions for $H(x)$, $\mathcal{H}(x)$, $\eta(x)$, $t(x)$ and all distance measures. A Metropolis MCMC is used to fit the luminosity distance to the Betoule et al. (2014) supernova sample ($N=31$ data points) via

$$\chi^2(h,\Omega_{M,0},\Omega_{k,0}) = \sum_{i=1}^N\frac{\left[d_L(z_i,h,\Omega_{M,0},\Omega_{k,0})-d_L^{\rm obs}(z_i)\right]^2}{\sigma_i^2}.$$
(107)

The RecombinationHistory class solves for $X_e$ using Saha when $X_e>0.99$ and Peebles’ equation otherwise, with $Y_p\ne0$. The reionization contributions (Equations 47–49) are evaluated once at each grid point (not fed back into the ODE, to avoid spurious accumulation). $\tau$ is integrated from $x=0$ backwards with $\tau(0)=0$.

The Perturbations class starts in tight coupling (only $\Theta_0$, $\Theta_1$; no polarization) and switches to the full system when $|\tau'|<10|\tau''|/|\tau'|$ or $|\tau'|<10$. Neutrino multipoles $N_\ell$ and photon polarization multipoles $\Theta_\ell^P$ are now evolved following the full hierarchy of Sect. 2. The source function $\tilde{S}(k,x)$ is stored for the line-of-sight integration.

The PowerSpectrum class computes $\Theta_\ell(k,x=0)$ for $2<\ell<1200$ using the ODE

$$\frac{d\Theta_\ell(k,x)}{dx} = \tilde{S}(k,x)\,j_\ell\!\left[k(\eta_0-\eta)\right],\qquad\Theta_\ell(k,-\infty)=0,$$
(108)

using spherical Bessel functions from the ComplexBessel library, and then integrates over $k$ to obtain $C_\ell$.

4.3. Evolution of structure

For all the main physical quantities in this subsection, four different scales (wave numbers) are presented: large scales with $k=0.001$ and $0.0001\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$; intermediate scales with $k=0.01\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$; and small scales with $k=0.1\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$.

In Figure 13 one can see the density perturbations for baryonic matter, cold dark matter and photons. The pressure-less CDM perturbations remain constant until gravity can start contracting them. The baryonic perturbations entering the horizon before the end of recombination oscillate as a driven harmonic oscillator, with gravity as the attracting force and radiation pressure as the repulsive force. After decoupling, the baryons fall into the dark matter potential wells.

In Figure 14 one can see the velocity perturbations for baryonic matter, cold dark matter and photons. The velocities of the CDM perturbations increase from close to zero until horizon entry. The baryonic matter velocities follow the oscillations, but with a phase shift of $\pi/2$ so that the minimum value of the velocity corresponds to the maximum size of the perturbation. For the photon velocities, the same behavior is seen, being phase shifted by $\pi/2$ as for the baryons.

In Figure 15 the photon quadrupoles can be seen, related with the polarization source term $\Pi = \Theta_2+\Theta_0^P+\Theta_2^P$ that sources the photon polarization multipoles $\Theta_\ell^P$, shown in Figure 16. As expected from the source term, $\Theta_\ell^P$ only becomes significant around and after recombination/decoupling, once $\Theta_2$ has grown large enough. The polarization multipoles are consistently smaller in amplitude than the corresponding temperature quadrupole, as expected since Thomson scattering only converts a fraction of the local temperature quadrupole into polarization.

Lastly, the gravitational potentials are presented in Figure 17. Here it is evident that $\Psi\approx-\Phi$, and thus we will keep to discussing $\Phi$ alone where it is inferred that $\Psi$ behaves similarly. Again all scales are constant before entering the horizon. On small scales, entering the horizon in the radiation dominated regime, the potentials are dominated by radiation energy, which does not cluster efficiently. Thus the potentials decay rapidly when the monopole increases, but start to oscillate due to the baryon-photon-fluid oscillations until after decoupling. When entering into the matter dominated regime, the dominating energy contributor to the potentials are baryons and the CDM, which do indeed cluster and causes the potentials to be constant with growing matter perturbations.

4.4. CMB power-spectrum

In Figure 18 the resulting multipoles $\Theta_\ell$ obtained from Equation (108) through the line-of-sight integration method are displayed, for four significantly different angular scales. Here it can be seen the oscillatory and dampening effect from the Bessel functions, as the transfer functions look like damped harmonic oscillators. The integrand from Equation (102) is shown in Figure 19. As it shows essentially the square of the transfer function, one will expect to see much of the same trends, with only positive values, with the larger scales even more dominant as it is divided by the wavenumber.

In Figure 20 the total matter power-spectrum can be seen. For large scales, late horizon entry results in more or less unprocessed perturbations, giving a spectrum proportional to $k^{n_s}\sim k^1$. Going to smaller scales the peak is hit at the equality scale $k_{\rm eq}$, the scale corresponding to perturbations entering the horizon at matter-radiation equality. For even smaller scales the Meszaros effect suppresses power as $k^{n_s-4}$.

The main result, the CMB power spectrum, is shown in Figure 21. Reading from large to small scales: the Sachs-Wolfe plateau on the largest scales; a late ISW enhancement at $\ell\lesssim10$; the acoustic peaks, with odd peaks (compression modes) enhanced by the baryon mass in the gravitational field and even peaks (rarefaction modes) suppressed; and exponential Silk (diffusion) damping at $\ell\gtrsim500$. In Figure 22, the contribution of each term to the final CMB power-spectrum can be seen. Comparing with Figure 6 in [16] (Figure 23 in this work) one can see that both plots look similar.

With photon polarization now included, the polarization (EE) and temperature-polarization cross (TE) power spectra can also be computed, shown in Figure 24. The TT power spectrum matches the observational data well: the ratio $D_\ell^{TT}(\text{with reionization})/D_\ell^{TT}(\text{without reionization})$ agrees with the expected $e^{-2\tau_{\rm reion}}$ suppression to the third decimal across the full $\ell$ range, with $\tau_{\rm reion}\sim0.058$, consistent with the Planck-measured value $0.054\pm0.007$ [6]. The EE and TE spectra show a known excess at low-to-intermediate $\ell$ attributable to the simplified (non-spin-2) polarization source term used here [7], which becomes negligible for $\ell\gtrsim700$–1000 and does not affect the main conclusions.

Additionally, a map of the CMB power-spectrum has been generated, being shown in Figure 25.

4.3. Evolution of structure

Four different scales are presented: large ($k=0.001$ and $0.0001\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$), intermediate ($k=0.01\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$), and small ($k=0.1\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$). The density perturbations (Figure 13) show CDM perturbations remaining constant until gravity begins contracting them, while baryon perturbations oscillate as a driven harmonic oscillator before decoupling, after which they fall into the dark matter potential wells. After decoupling, photons are free to move unhindered and the damping of the harmonic oscillations causes the perturbations to eventually become more or less constant, until the dark energy dominated regime when expansion causes them to decay to zero.

4.4. CMB power-spectrum

The matter power-spectrum (Figure 20) shows the spectrum going as $k^{n_s}\sim k^1$ on large scales and being suppressed as $k^{n_s-4}$ by the Meszaros effect on small scales, with a peak at the equality scale $k_{\rm eq}$.

The main result, the CMB power spectrum, is shown in Figure 21. Reading from large to small scales: the Sachs-Wolfe plateau on the largest scales; a late ISW enhancement at $\ell\lesssim10$ from the dark energy dominated regime; the acoustic peaks, with odd peaks (compression modes) enhanced by the baryon mass in the gravitational field; and exponential Silk (diffusion) damping at $\ell\gtrsim500$. With photon polarization included, the TT spectrum ratio $D_\ell^{TT}(\text{with reionization})/D_\ell^{TT}(\text{without reionization})$ agrees with the expected $e^{-2\tau_{\rm reion}}$ suppression to the third decimal across the full $\ell$ range, with $\tau_{\rm reion}\sim0.058$, consistent with the Planck-measured value $0.054\pm0.007$ [6]. The EE and TE spectra show a known excess at low-to-intermediate $\ell$ attributable to the simplified (non-spin-2) polarization source term used here, which becomes negligible for $\ell\gtrsim700$–1000 and does not affect the main conclusions.

5. Conclusions

Background cosmology of the Universe has been solved and each epoch has been determined, identifying in each of them the dominating substance. The age of the Universe has also been computed, being this $t_{\rm Universe}\approx13.86\,\text{Gyr}$.

Recombination history of the Universe has been solved, now including a non-zero Helium fraction and hydrogen/Helium reionization, computing different important times during recombination, summarized in Table 3. The sound horizon at decoupling has also been computed, being this $r_s\approx112.309\,\text{Mpc}$.

The evolution of the structure in the Universe has been solved and the different kind of perturbations have been determined, including now the neutrino and photon polarization perturbations, showing that the metric in our Universe deviates from the Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW), implying that our Universe is not homogeneous nor isotropic.

The matter and CMB power-spectra have been computed with high accuracy, now including neutrinos, Helium, reionization and photon polarization. The TT spectrum reproduces the observational data and the expected $e^{-2\tau_{\rm reion}}$ suppression from reionization very precisely; the EE and TE spectra show a known, well-diagnosed excess of power at low $\ell$ (traced to the simplified, non-spin-2 polarization source term used here), which does not affect the main conclusions of this work.
