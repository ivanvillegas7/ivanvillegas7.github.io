Course project · AST5220 Cosmology II · University of Oslo

Computing the CMB power spectrum

Iván Villegas-Pérez — Supervisors: Prof. David F. Mota & Dr. Hans A. Winther (UiO)

Read PDF ↗ GitHub ↗

Context. The goal of this whole study is to be able to predict the CMB (and matter) fluctuations (described by the so-called power-spectrum) from first principles and learn about all the different physical processes that goes on to be able to explain the results.

Aims. The goals in this work are to make a class/module that takes in the cosmological parameters and has functions for getting the Hubble parameter, conformal time and distance measures as function of scale factor and the variable $x = \ln a$, to make a class/module that takes in the abundance of Helium in the Universe and has functions for getting the free electron fraction (including both hydrogen and Helium recombination, and hydrogen and Helium reionization), the optical depth (and its derivatives) and the visibility function (and its derivatives), to make a class/module that evolves the density, velocity, temperature, polarization and neutrino perturbations from an early time until today, and to make a class/module that computes the CMB power-spectrum including the contributions from massless neutrinos and photon polarization.

Methods. Four codes have been developed, using the template given by Hans A. Winther. In the first code, several functions have been implemented to solve the background cosmology of the Universe and plot the results, identifying each epoch domination. In the second code, several functions have been implemented to solve the recombination history of the Universe, now including a non-zero Helium fraction and a hydrogen/Helium reionization model. In the third one, several functions have been implemented to evolve the density, velocity, potential, photon temperature and polarization, and neutrino perturbations. Lastly, in the fourth code, several functions have been implemented to compute the CMB power-spectrum, including the neutrino and polarization source terms.

Results. Background cosmology of the Universe has been solved and each epoch has been determined. The age of the Universe has also been computed, being this $t_{\rm Universe} \approx 13.86$ Gyr. Recombination history of the Universe has been solved, now including Helium and reionization. The sound horizon at decoupling has been computed, being this $r_s \approx 112.309$ Mpc. Evolution of the structure of the Universe has been solved, including neutrino and photon polarization perturbations. The CMB power-spectrum has been computed with the full physics (neutrinos, Helium, reionization and polarization), along with a CMB map.

Conclusions. The CMB power-spectrum has been computed with a high accuracy, now including neutrinos, Helium, reionization and photon polarization, considering the approximations made.

Key words. background cosmology – evolution of the Universe – epoch domination – recombination – structure evolution – perturbations – CMB power-spectrum – CMB map

1. Introduction

The goals of this study have been to solve the evolution of the uniform background cosmology in the Universe, to solve its recombination history, to solve the evolution of structure in the Universe and to compute the CMB power-spectrum [1].

For this first goal, a class/module has been implemented. This class takes the given cosmological parameters and (with the use of different functions) returns the Hubble parameter ($H$), conformal time ($\eta$) and distance measures as function of the scale factor ($a$) and the variable $x$ ($x = \ln a$), which will be the main time variable for this work [2]. For the second goal, a different class/module computes the optical depth ($\tau$) as a function of $x$ and its derivatives, and the visibility function $\tilde{g}$ and its derivatives [3]. For the third goal, two-dimensional functions (of $x$ and $k$) for each of the main physical quantities of interest are constructed [4]. Finally, the CMB and matter power spectrum has been computed [5].

This work extends an earlier, simplified version of the code (which neglected neutrinos, Helium, reionization and photon polarization) by including all four of these ingredients, together with several bug fixes. The most significant were: a sign error in the Helium-reionization redshift ($z = e^{-x}-1$ instead of $e^x-1$); a normalization bug where the reionization contribution to $X_e$ was accumulated by the Peebles ODE instead of being evaluated once per grid point; a missing $\sqrt{(\ell+2)!/(\ell-2)!}$ prefactor in the polarization source term; and a spurious $|\cdot|$ in the line-of-sight $C_\ell$ integral that suppressed the sign changes of the TE cross-spectrum.

The fiducial cosmology is the best-fit from Planck 2018 [6], now including neutrinos, Helium, reionization and photon polarization: $h_0 = 0.67$, $T_{\rm CMB,0} = 2.7255\,\text{K}$, $N_{\rm eff} = 3.046$, $\Omega_{B,0} = 0.05$, $\Omega_{\rm CDM,0} = 0.267$, $\Omega_{k,0} = 0$, $Y_p = 0.245$, $z_{\rm reion} = 8$, $\Delta z_{\rm reion} = 0.5$, $z_{\rm He,reion} = 3.5$, $\Delta z_{\rm He,reion} = 0.5$, $n_s = 0.965$, $A_s = 2.1\cdot10^{-9}$.

2. Basic cosmology

2.1. Background Cosmology

The Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker metric for a flat space ($k=0$):

$$ds^2 = -c^2\,dt^2 + a^2(t)\!\left[dr^2 + r^2\!\left(d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2\right)\right],$$
(1)

where $a(t)$ is the scale factor ($a_0=1$ today) and $\eta$ is the conformal time. We use $x \equiv \ln a$ as the main time variable and the redshift $1+z = a_0/a(t)$. The Friedmann equation:

$$H = H_0\sqrt{\Omega_{M,0}\,a^{-3}+\Omega_{R,0}\,a^{-4}+\Omega_{k,0}\,a^{-2}+\Omega_{\Lambda,0}},$$
(4)

where $H\equiv\dot{a}/a$, $\Omega_{M,0}=\Omega_{B,0}+\Omega_{\rm CDM,0}$ and $\Omega_{R,0}=\Omega_{\nu,0}+\Omega_{\gamma,0}$. The scaled Hubble parameter is $\mathcal{H}\equiv aH$. Each component evolves as:

$$\rho_{\rm CDM}=\rho_{\rm CDM,0}\,a^{-3},\quad\rho_B=\rho_{B,0}\,a^{-3},\quad\rho_\gamma=\rho_{\gamma,0}\,a^{-4},\quad\rho_\nu=\rho_{\nu,0}\,a^{-4},\quad\rho_\Lambda=\rho_{\Lambda,0}.$$
(6–10)

The radiation densities follow from the observed CMB temperature:

$$\Omega_{\gamma,0}=2\cdot\frac{\pi^2}{30}\frac{(k_BT_{\rm CMB,0})^4}{\hbar^3c^5}\cdot\frac{8\pi G}{3H_0^2},\qquad\Omega_{\nu,0}=N_{\rm eff}\cdot\left(\frac{7}{8}\cdot\frac{4}{11}\right)^{4/3}\Omega_{\gamma,0}.$$
(17–18)

The conformal time satisfies:

$$\frac{d\eta}{dx}=\frac{c}{\mathcal{H}},$$
(22)

solved with initial condition $\eta(x_{\rm start})=c/\mathcal{H}(x_{\rm start})$. For a flat Universe: $r=\eta_0-\eta$, $d_A=ar$, $d_L=r/a^2$. The cosmic time satisfies $dt/dx=1/H(x)$.

2.2. Recombination History

The optical depth:

$$\tau(\eta)=\int_\eta^{\eta_0}n_e\sigma_T a\,d\eta\prime,\qquad\tau\prime=\frac{d\tau}{dx}=-\frac{cn_e\sigma_T}{\mathcal{H}}.$$
(32–33)

The fractional electron density $X_e\equiv n_e/n_H$, with $n_H=(1-Y_p)\Omega_{B,0}\rho_{c,0}/(m_H a^3)$. The Saha equation (when $X_e>0.99$):

$$\frac{X_e^2}{1-X_e}=\frac{1}{n_B}\left(\frac{m_eT_B}{2\pi}\right)^{3/2}e^{-\epsilon_0/T_B},$$
(36)

where $\epsilon_0=13.6\,\text{eV}$. The Peebles\' equation (when $X_e<0.99$):

$$\frac{dX_e}{dx}=\frac{C_r(T_b)}{H}\!\left[\beta(T_b)(1-X_e)-n_H\alpha^{(2)}(T_b)X_e^2\right].$$
(37)

2.2.1. Reionization

A phenomenological tanh reionization model is added. For hydrogen reionization:

$$X_e^{H,\rm reion}(x)=\frac{f_H}{2}\!\left[1+\tanh\!\left(\frac{y_{\rm reion}-y}{\Delta y_{\rm reion}}\right)\right],$$
(47)

where $f_H=1+Y_p/[4(1-Y_p)]$, $y=(1+z)^{3/2}$. For the second Helium reionization:

$$X_e^{He,\rm reion}(x)=\frac{f_{He}}{2}\!\left[1+\tanh\!\left(\frac{z_{He,\rm reion}-z}{\Delta z_{He,\rm reion}}\right)\right],\quad f_{He}=\frac{Y_p}{4(1-Y_p)}.$$
(48)

The total free electron fraction: $X_e(x)=X_e^{\rm Saha/Peebles}(x)+X_e^{H,\rm reion}(x)+X_e^{He,\rm reion}(x)$.

The visibility function $\tilde{g}(x)=-\tau\prime(x)e^{-\tau(x)}$ satisfies $\int_{-\infty}^0\tilde{g}(x)dx=1$ and peaks sharply at $x\approx-7$ ($z\sim1100$). The sound horizon at decoupling: $ds/dx=c_s/\mathcal{H}$, $c_s=c\sqrt{R/[3(1+R)]}$, $R=4\Omega_{\gamma,0}/(3\Omega_{B,0}a)$.

2.3. Perturbations

The linearized Einstein and Boltzmann equations are solved numerically. At early times the tight-coupling approximation is used; at later times the line-of-sight integration approach of Zaldarriaga and Seljak [10] is used with $\ell_{\rm max}=8$ in the perturbation stage.

2.3.1. The full system

Photon temperature multipoles ($\Pi=\Theta_2+\Theta_0^P+\Theta_2^P$, $R=4\Omega_{\gamma,0}/(3\Omega_{B,0}a)$):

$$\Theta_0\prime=-\frac{ck}{\mathcal{H}}\Theta_1-\Phi\prime,$$
(52)
$$\Theta_1\prime=\frac{ck}{3\mathcal{H}}\Theta_0-\frac{2ck}{3\mathcal{H}}\Theta_2+\frac{ck}{3\mathcal{H}}\Psi+\tau\prime\!\left[\Theta_1+\frac{1}{3}v_b\right],$$
(53)
$$\Theta_\ell\prime=\frac{\ell ck}{(2\ell+1)\mathcal{H}}\Theta_{\ell-1}-\frac{(\ell+1)ck}{(2\ell+1)\mathcal{H}}\Theta_{\ell+1}+\tau\prime\!\left[\Theta_\ell-\frac{\Pi\delta_{\ell,2}}{10}\right],\quad 2\le\ell<\ell_{\rm max}.$$
(54)

Photon polarization multipoles:

$$\Theta_0^{P\prime}=-\frac{ck}{\mathcal{H}}\Theta_1^P+\tau\prime\!\left[\Theta_0^P-\frac{1}{2}\Pi\right],\qquad\Theta_\ell^{P\prime}=\frac{\ell ck}{(2\ell+1)\mathcal{H}}\Theta_{\ell-1}^P-\frac{(\ell+1)ck}{(2\ell+1)\mathcal{H}}\Theta_{\ell+1}^P+\tau\prime\!\left[\Theta_\ell^P-\frac{\Pi\delta_{\ell,2}}{10}\right].$$
(56–57)

Neutrino multipoles:

$$N_0\prime=-\frac{ck}{\mathcal{H}}N_1-\Phi\prime,\qquad N_1\prime=\frac{ck}{3\mathcal{H}}N_0-\frac{2ck}{3\mathcal{H}}N_2+\frac{ck}{3\mathcal{H}}\Psi,\qquad N_\ell\prime=\frac{\ell ck}{(2\ell+1)\mathcal{H}}N_{\ell-1}-\frac{(\ell+1)ck}{(2\ell+1)\mathcal{H}}N_{\ell+1}.$$
(59–61)

Cold dark matter and baryons:

$$\delta_{\rm CDM}\prime=\frac{ck}{\mathcal{H}}v_{\rm CDM}-3\Phi\prime,\quad v_{\rm CDM}\prime=-v_{\rm CDM}-\frac{ck}{\mathcal{H}}\Psi,\quad\delta_B\prime=\frac{ck}{\mathcal{H}}v_B-3\Phi\prime,\quad v_B\prime=-v_B-\frac{ck}{\mathcal{H}}\Psi+\tau\prime R(3\Theta_1+v_B).$$
(63–66)

Metric perturbations:

$$\Phi\prime=\frac{H_0^2}{2\mathcal{H}^2}\!\left[\frac{\Omega_{\rm CDM,0}}{a}\delta_{\rm CDM}+\frac{\Omega_{B,0}}{a}\delta_B+4\frac{\Omega_{\gamma,0}}{a^2}\Theta_0+4\frac{\Omega_{\nu,0}}{a^2}N_0\right]+\Psi-\frac{c^2k^2}{3\mathcal{H}^2}\Phi,$$
(67)
$$\Psi=-\Phi-\frac{12H_0^2}{c^2k^2a^2}\!\left[\Omega_{\gamma,0}\Theta_2+\Omega_{\nu,0}N_2\right].$$
(68)

2.3.2. The tight coupling regime

In tight coupling only $\Theta_0$ and $\Theta_1$ are evolved. Higher-order moments: $\Theta_2=-8ck/(15\mathcal{H}\tau\prime)\,\Theta_1$ (with polarization), $\Theta_\ell=-[\ell/(2\ell+1)]\,[ck/(\mathcal{H}\tau\prime)]\,\Theta_{\ell-1}$ for $\ell>2$, $\Theta_0^P=\frac{5}{4}\Theta_2$, $\Theta_1^P=-[ck/(4\mathcal{H}\tau\prime)]\Theta_2$, $\Theta_2^P=\frac{1}{4}\Theta_2$.

2.3.3. Initial conditions

Deep in tight coupling, with $f_\nu=\Omega_{\nu,0}/(\Omega_{\gamma,0}+\Omega_{\nu,0})$:

$$\Psi=-\frac{1}{\tfrac{2}{3}+\tfrac{2}{5}f_\nu},\quad\Phi=-\!\left(1+\frac{2f_\nu}{5}\right)\Psi,\quad\delta_{\rm CDM}=\delta_B=-\frac{3}{2}\Psi,\quad v_{\rm CDM}=v_B=-\frac{ck}{2\mathcal{H}}\Psi,$$
(80–85)
$$\Theta_0=-\frac{1}{2}\Psi,\quad\Theta_1=\frac{ck}{6\mathcal{H}}\Psi,\quad N_0=-\frac{1}{2}\Psi,\quad N_1=\frac{ck}{6\mathcal{H}}\Psi,\quad N_2=-\frac{c^2k^2a^2(\Phi+\Psi)}{12H_0^2\Omega_{\nu,0}}.$$
(84–94)

2.4. CMB power-spectrum

The CMB power spectrum $C_\ell\equiv\langle|a_{\ell m}|^2\rangle$. Using the line-of-sight integration method [10]:

$$\Theta_\ell(k,x=0)=\int_{-\infty}^0\tilde{S}(k,x)\,j_\ell\!\left[k(\eta_0-\eta)\right]dx,$$
(98)

where the source function is

$$\tilde{S}(k,x)=\tilde{g}\!\left[\Theta_0+\Psi+\frac{1}{4}\Pi\right]+e^{-\tau}(\Psi\prime-\Phi\prime)-\frac{1}{ck}\frac{d}{dx}(\mathcal{H}\tilde{g}v_b)+\frac{3}{4c^2k^2}\frac{d}{dx}\!\left[\mathcal{H}\frac{d}{dx}(\mathcal{H}\tilde{g}\Pi)\right].$$
(99)

The four terms are: (1) monopole/Sachs-Wolfe, (2) Integrated Sachs-Wolfe, (3) Doppler, (4) polarization/quadrupole correction. The CMB power spectrum:

$$C_\ell=4\pi\int_0^\infty A_s\!\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm pivot}}\right)^{n_s-1}\Theta_\ell^2(k)\,\frac{dk}{k}.$$
(102)

The matter power-spectrum: $P(k,x)=|\Delta_M(k,x)|^2 P_{\rm prim}(k)$, $\Delta_M(k,x)\equiv c^2k^2\Phi(k,x)/[\frac{3}{2}\Omega_{M,0}a^{-1}H_0^2]$.

3. Implementation method

Four C++ classes have been implemented (all code publicly available on GitHub): BackgroundCosmology, RecombinationHistory, Perturbations and PowerSpectrum.

The BackgroundCosmology class computes $\Omega_{\gamma,0}$, $\Omega_{\nu,0}$, $\Omega_{\Lambda,0}$ and provides splined functions for $H(x)$, $\mathcal{H}(x)$, $\eta(x)$, $t(x)$ and all distance measures. A Metropolis MCMC fits the luminosity distance to the Betoule et al. (2014) supernova sample ($N=31$ data points):

$$\chi^2(h,\Omega_{M,0},\Omega_{k,0})=\sum_{i=1}^N\frac{\left[d_L(z_i,h,\Omega_{M,0},\Omega_{k,0})-d_L^{\rm obs}(z_i)\right]^2}{\sigma_i^2}.$$
(107)

The RecombinationHistory class solves for $X_e$ using Saha when $X_e>0.99$ and Peebles otherwise, with $Y_p\ne0$. Reionization contributions are evaluated once per grid point (not fed back into the ODE). The Perturbations class starts in tight coupling and switches to the full system when $|\tau\prime|<10|\tau\prime\prime|/|\tau\prime|$ or $|\tau\prime|<10$. The PowerSpectrum class computes $\Theta_\ell(k,x=0)$ for $2<\ell<1200$ via:

$$\frac{d\Theta_\ell(k,x)}{dx}=\tilde{S}(k,x)\,j_\ell\!\left[k(\eta_0-\eta)\right],\qquad\Theta_\ell(k,-\infty)=0,$$
(108)

using spherical Bessel functions from the ComplexBessel library.

4.3. Evolution of structure

For all the main physical quantities, four different wave numbers are presented: large scales ($k=0.001$ and $0.0001\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$), intermediate ($k=0.01\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$), and small ($k=0.1\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$).

In Figure 13 the density perturbations for baryonic matter, cold dark matter and photons are shown. CDM perturbations remain constant until gravity can start contracting them. Baryonic perturbations entering the horizon before the end of recombination oscillate as a driven harmonic oscillator, with gravity as the attracting force and radiation pressure as the repulsive force. After decoupling, the baryons fall into the dark matter potential wells.

In Figure 14 the velocity perturbations are shown. CDM velocities increase from close to zero until horizon entry. Baryonic matter velocities follow the oscillations with a phase shift of $\pi/2$, and the photon velocities follow similarly.

In Figure 15 the photon quadrupoles can be seen, related with the polarization source term $\Pi=\Theta_2+\Theta_0^P+\Theta_2^P$ that sources the photon polarization multipoles $\Theta_\ell^P$, shown in Figure 16. $\Theta_\ell^P$ only becomes significant around and after recombination, once $\Theta_2$ has grown large enough. The polarization multipoles are consistently smaller in amplitude than the corresponding temperature quadrupole, as expected since Thomson scattering only converts a fraction of the local temperature quadrupole into polarization.

Lastly, the gravitational potentials are presented in Figure 17. It is evident that $\Psi\approx-\Phi$. All scales are constant before entering the horizon. On small scales, entering in the radiation dominated regime, the potentials decay rapidly when the monopole increases, then start to oscillate due to the baryon-photon-fluid oscillations until after decoupling. In matter domination the dominating contributors to the potentials are baryons and CDM, which cluster and cause the potentials to be constant with growing matter perturbations.

4.4. CMB power-spectrum

In Figure 18 the resulting multipoles $\Theta_\ell$ obtained from Equation (108) through the line-of-sight integration method are displayed for four angular scales. The integrand from Equation (102) is shown in Figure 19.

In Figure 20 the total matter power-spectrum can be seen. For large scales the spectrum goes as $k^{n_s}\sim k^1$. Going to smaller scales the peak is hit at the equality scale $k_{\rm eq}$. For even smaller scales the Meszaros effect suppresses power as $k^{n_s-4}$.

The main result, the CMB power spectrum, is shown in Figure 21. Reading from large to small scales: the Sachs-Wolfe plateau; a late ISW enhancement at $\ell\lesssim10$; the acoustic peaks with odd peaks (compression modes) enhanced by the baryon mass; and Silk (diffusion) damping at $\ell\gtrsim500$. In Figure 22, the contribution of each term to the final CMB power-spectrum can be seen.

With photon polarization now included, the EE and TE power spectra can also be computed, shown in Figure 24. The TT power spectrum matches the observational data well: the ratio $D_\ell^{TT}(\text{with reionization})/D_\ell^{TT}(\text{without reionization})$ agrees with the expected $e^{-2\tau_{\rm reion}}$ suppression to the third decimal, with $\tau_{\rm reion}\sim0.058$, consistent with the Planck-measured value $0.054\pm0.007$ [6]. The EE and TE spectra show a known excess at low-to-intermediate $\ell$ attributable to the simplified (non-spin-2) polarization source term [7], which becomes negligible for $\ell\gtrsim700$–1000.

Additionally, a CMB map has been generated using Healpy from the theoretical $C_\ell$ spectrum (Figure 25).

5. Conclusions

Background cosmology of the Universe has been solved and each epoch has been determined. The age of the Universe has been computed: $t_{\rm Universe}\approx13.86\,\text{Gyr}$.

Recombination history has been solved, now including a non-zero Helium fraction and hydrogen/Helium reionization. The sound horizon at decoupling is $r_s\approx112.309\,\text{Mpc}$.

The evolution of structure has been solved, including neutrino and photon polarization perturbations, showing that the metric in our Universe deviates from the FLRW metric, implying that our Universe is not homogeneous nor isotropic.

The matter and CMB power-spectra have been computed with high accuracy, including neutrinos, Helium, reionization and photon polarization. The TT spectrum reproduces the observational data and the expected $e^{-2\tau_{\rm reion}}$ suppression precisely; the EE and TE spectra show a known, well-diagnosed excess at low $\ell$ (traced to the simplified, non-spin-2 polarization source term), which does not affect the main conclusions.
