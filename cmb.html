Course project · AST5220 Cosmology II · University of Oslo · 5 June 2024

Computing the CMB power spectrum

Iván Villegas-Pérez — Astronomy & Astrophysics manuscript style · June 5, 2024

Read PDF ↗ GitHub ↗

Context. The goal of this whole study is to be able to predict the CMB (and matter) fluctuations (described by the so-called power-spectrum) from first principles and learn about all the different physical processes that goes on to be able to explain the results.

Aims. The goals in this work are to make a class/module that takes in the cosmological parameters and has functions for getting the Hubble parameter, conformal time and distance measures as function of scale factor and the variable $x = \ln a$, to make a class/module that takes in the abundance of Helium in the Universe and has functions for getting the free electron fraction, the optical depth (and its derivatives) and the visibility function (and its derivatives), to make a class/module that evolves the density, velocity, temperature, polarization and neutrinos perturbations from an early time until today, and to make a class/module that computes the CMB power-spectrum.

Methods. Four codes have been developed, using the template given by Hans A. Winther. In the first code, several functions have been implemented to solve the background cosmology of the Universe and plot the results, identifying each epoch domination (either relativistic particles, non-relativistic matter or dark energy). In the second code, several functions have been implemented to solve the recombination history of the Universe and plot the results. In the third one, several functions have been implemented to evolve the perturbations and plot the results. Lastly, in the fourth code, several functions have been implemented to compute the CMB power-spectrum and plot the results.

Results. Background cosmology of the Universe has been solved and each epoch has been determined, identifying in each of them the dominating substance. The age of the Universe has also been computed, being this $t_{\rm Universe} \approx 13.86$ Gyr. Recombination history of the Universe has been solved. The relevant times of the Universe’s recombination history have been computed, as well as the sound horizon at decoupling, being this $r_s \approx 128.177$ Mpc. Evolution of the structure of the Universe has been solved. The density and velocity perturbations for CDM, baryonic matter and photon have been computed. The CMB power-spectrum has been computed, along with a CMB map.

Conclusions. The CMB power-spectrum has been computed with a high accuracy, considering the approximations made.

Key words. background cosmology – evolution of the Universe – epoch domination – recombination – structure evolution – perturbations – CMB power-spectrum – CMB map

1. Introduction

The goals of this study have been to solve the evolution of the uniform background cosmology in the Universe, to solve its recombination history, to solve the evolution of structure in the Universe and to compute the CMB power-spectrum.1

For this first goal, a class/module has been implemented. This class takes the given cosmological parameters and (with the use of different functions) returns the Hubble parameter ($H$), conformal time ($\eta$) and distance measures as function of the scale factor ($a$) and the variable $x$ ($x = \ln a$), which will be the main time variable for this work.2

For the second goal, a different class/module has been implemented. The final goal of this part is to compute the optical depth ($\tau$) as a function of $x$ and its derivatives, and the visibility function, $\tilde{g}$, and its derivatives.3

For the third goal, a different class/module has been implemented. The final goal of this part is to construct two-dimensional functions (of time and Fourier scale, $x$ and $k$) for each of the main physical quantities of interest: $\Phi(x,k)$, $\Psi(x,k)$, $\delta_{\rm CDM}(x,k)$, $\delta_b(x,k)$, $v_{\rm CDM}(x,k)$, $v_B(x,k)$ and $\Theta_\ell(x,k)$.4

Finally, after spending a lot of time on understanding the evolution of the background properties of the universe, its ionization history, and the growth of structure in the universe, the key statistical observable in cosmology has been computed: the CMB and matter power spectrum!5

The fiducial cosmology used in this work is the best-fit cosmology found from fits to Planck 2018 data [Aghanim et al. (2020)] without neutrinos, Helium, reionization nor photon polarization:

$h_0 = 0.67$,
$T_{\rm CMB,0} = 2.7255$ K,
$N_{\rm eff} = 3.046$,
$\Omega_{B,0} = 0.05$,
$\Omega_{\rm CDM,0} = 0.267$,
$\Omega_{k,0} = -kc^2/H_0^2 = 0$,
$\Omega_{\Lambda,0} = 1 - (\Omega_{B,0} + \Omega_{\rm CDM,0} + \Omega_{k,0} + \Omega_{\nu,0} + \Omega_{\gamma,0})$,
$Y_p = 0$,
$n_s = 0.965$,
$A_s = 2.1 \cdot 10^{-9}$.

2. Basic cosmology

2.1. Background Cosmology

Let’s review the theory basics. The goal of this work has been computing the expansion history of the universe, and look at the uniform background densities of the various matter and energy components. Let’s first define the Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker metric (here for a flat space where $k=0$),

$$ds^2 = -c^2 dt^2 + a^2(t)\!\left[dr^2 + r^2\!\left(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2\right)\right],$$
(1)

or in Cartesian coordinates

$$ds^2 = a^2(t)\!\left(d\eta^2 + dx^2 + dy^2 + dz^2\right),$$
(2)

where $a(t)$ is the scale factor, which measures the size of the universe relative to today ($a_0 = a_{\rm today} = 1$), and $\eta$ is called conformal time. As we will be looking at phenomena that varies strongly over a wide range of time scales, we will mostly be using the logarithm of the scale factor, $x \equiv \ln a$, as the main time variable. A fifth time variable is the redshift, $z$, which is defined as

$$1 + z = a_0/a(t).$$
(3)

The relevant equation for this work purposes is the Friedmann equation, which may be written (when $k=0$ is not assumed) on the following form

$$H = H_0\sqrt{\Omega_{M,0}\,a^{-3} + \Omega_{R,0}\,a^{-4} + \Omega_{k,0}\,a^{-2} + \Omega_{\Lambda,0}},$$
(4)

where $H \equiv \dot{a}/a$ is the Hubble parameter (dot denotes derivatives with respect to physical time, $\dot{\;}=d/dt$), and $\Omega_{B,0}$, $\Omega_{\rm CDM,0}$, $\Omega_{\gamma,0}$, $\Omega_{\nu,0}$, and $\Omega_{\Lambda,0}$ are the present day relative densities of baryonic (ordinary) matter, dark matter, radiation, neutrinos and dark energy, respectively. The terms $\Omega_{M,0}=\Omega_{B,0}+\Omega_{\rm CDM,0}$ and $\Omega_{R,0}=\Omega_{\nu,0}+\Omega_{\gamma,0}$ stand for cold (non-relativistic) matter and relativistic particles (neutrinos and photons). This gives us:

$$\rho_{\rm CDM} = \rho_{\rm CDM,0}\,a^{-3},\qquad \rho_B = \rho_{B,0}\,a^{-3},\qquad \rho_\gamma = \rho_{\gamma,0}\,a^{-4},\qquad \rho_\nu = \rho_{\nu,0}\,a^{-4},\qquad \rho_\Lambda = \rho_{\Lambda,0}.$$
(5–10)

Two of the density parameters above follow from the observed temperature of the CMB. We have that $\Omega_{\gamma,0}$ and $\Omega_{\nu,0}$ are given by

$$\Omega_{\gamma,0} = 2\cdot\frac{\pi^2 k_B T_{\rm CMB,0}}{30}\frac{1}{\hbar^3 c^5}\frac{8\pi G}{3H_0^2},$$
(17)
$$\Omega_{\nu,0} = N_{\rm eff}\cdot\left(\frac{7}{8}\frac{4}{11}\right)^{4/3}\Omega_{\gamma,0},$$
(18)

where $T_{\rm CMB,0}=2.7255$ K is the temperature of the CMB today and $N_{\rm eff}=3.046$ is the effective number of massless neutrinos. We define matter-radiation equality as the time when $\Omega_M = \Omega_R$ and the matter-dark energy transition as the time when $\Omega_M = \Omega_\Lambda$. The onset of acceleration is the time when $\ddot{a}=0$.

To find a computable expression for $\eta$, note that $d\eta/dt = c/a$. This leads to the ODE

$$\frac{d\eta}{dx} = \frac{c}{\mathcal{H}},$$
(22)

where $\mathcal{H}=aH$ is the conformal Hubble parameter. The initial condition is $\eta(x_{\rm start}) = c/\mathcal{H}(x_{\rm start})$. For a flat Universe, all the standard distance measures follow from the conformal time. The angular diameter distance is $d_A = ar$, and the luminosity distance is $d_L = r/a^2$, where $r = \eta_0 - \eta$ for $\Omega_{k,0}=0$.

The relation between cosmic time $t$ and $x$ follows from $dt/dx = 1/H(x)$. Evaluating this at $x=0$ gives the age of the Universe.

2.2. Recombination History

The problem of this work is to compute the optical depth, $\tau(x)$, and the so-called visibility function, $g(x)$, which is needed for integrating the Boltzmann-Einstein equations. Light that travels through a medium can be absorbed by the medium. The quantity $\tau$ is called the optical depth. The transition between the Universe being optically thick and optically thin happens around recombination. The optical depth is defined as

$$\tau(\eta) = \int_\eta^{\eta_0} n_e\,\sigma_T\,a\,d\eta',$$
(32)

which in differential form reads

$$\tau' = \frac{d\tau}{dx} = -\frac{c\,n_e\,\sigma_T}{\mathcal{H}}.$$
(33)

There are two different equations available for $X_e$ as a function of temperature and density, namely the so-called Saha and Peebles’ equations. If we ignore Helium then the Saha equation reads

$$\frac{X_e^2}{1-X_e} = \frac{1}{n_B}\left(\frac{m_e T_B}{2\pi}\right)^{3/2} e^{-\epsilon_0/T_B},$$
(36)

where $T_B$ is the baryon temperature and $\epsilon_0 = 13.6$ eV is the ionization energy of hydrogen. The Peebles’ equation reads

$$\frac{dX_e}{dx} = \frac{C_r(T_b)}{H}\!\left[\beta(T_b)(1-X_e) - n_H\,\alpha^{(2)}(T_b)\,X_e^2\right],$$
(37)

where the various coefficients $C_r$, $\Lambda_{2s\to 1s}$, $\Lambda_\alpha$, $\beta$, $\beta^{(2)}$, $\alpha^{(2)}$ and $\phi_2$ are given by Equations (38)–(46) of the report. When $X_e > 0.99$ the Saha equation is used; when $X_e < 0.99$ we switch to Peebles’.

The final item to compute is the so-called visibility function,

$$\tilde{g}(x) = -\tau'(x)\,e^{-\tau(x)},$$
(47)

which has the property that $\int_{-\infty}^0 \tilde{g}(x)\,dx = 1$ and is sharply peaked around $x=-7$, corresponding to redshifts $z \sim 1100$. The sound horizon at decoupling is

$$\frac{ds}{dx} = \frac{c_s}{\mathcal{H}},\qquad c_s = \frac{c}{\sqrt{3(1+R)}},\qquad R = \frac{4\Omega_{\gamma,0}}{3\Omega_{B,0}\,a},$$
(48)

and evaluating at $x=x_{\rm decoupling}$ gives $r_s \equiv s(x_{\rm decoupling})$.

2.3. Perturbations

During the lectures,9 the linearized Einstein and Boltzmann equations for photons, baryons and dark matter, and their respective inflationary initial conditions have been derived. One of the goals of the current study is to solve these equations numerically.

At early times the optical depth $\tau$ is very large (tight coupling), making the ODE system numerically unstable. The solution is to use a proper approximation in the tight coupling regime. At later times, the fluid becomes thinner and higher multipoles $\Theta_\ell$ become relevant. Fortunately, the line-of-sight integration approach of Zaldarriaga and Seljak [Seljak & Zaldarriaga (1996)] means only a small number of these ($\ell_{\rm max}=8$ in the perturbation stage) need to be included.

2.3.1. The full system

Photon temperature multipoles ($\Theta_\ell$), photon polarization multipoles ($\Theta^P_\ell$), neutrino multipoles ($N_\ell$), CDM and baryons, and metric perturbations are evolved according to Equations (49)–(65) of the report, which include:

$$\Theta_0' = -\frac{ck}{\mathcal{H}}\Theta_1 - \Phi',$$
(49)
$$\Theta_1' = \frac{ck}{3\mathcal{H}}\Theta_0 - \frac{2ck}{3\mathcal{H}}\Theta_2 + \frac{ck}{3\mathcal{H}}\Psi + \tau'\!\left(\Theta_1 + \frac{1}{3}v_b\right),$$
(50)
$$\delta_{\rm CDM}' = \frac{ck}{\mathcal{H}}v_{\rm CDM} - 3\Phi', \qquad v_{\rm CDM}' = -v_{\rm CDM} - \frac{ck}{\mathcal{H}}\Psi,$$
(60–61)
$$\Phi' = \frac{H_0^2}{2\mathcal{H}^2}\!\left[\frac{\Omega_{\rm CDM,0}}{a}\delta_{\rm CDM}+\frac{\Omega_{B,0}}{a}\delta_B+4\frac{\Omega_{\gamma,0}}{a^2}\Theta_0+4\frac{\Omega_{\nu,0}}{a^2}N_0\right]+\Psi-\frac{c^2k^2}{3\mathcal{H}^2}\Phi,$$
(64)
$$\Psi = -\Phi - \frac{12H_0^2}{c^2k^2a^2}\!\left[\Omega_{\gamma,0}\Theta_2+\Omega_{\nu,0}N_2\right].$$
(65)

2.3.2. The tight coupling regime

In the tight coupling regime, only $\Theta_0$ and $\Theta_1$ are evolved and the higher moments are given by the same relations as in the initial conditions. The expressions for $\Theta_1'$ and $v_B'$ become more involved (Equations 66–70) involving the quantity

$$q = -\frac{\text{Num.}}{\text{Den.}}$$
(68)

where Num. and Den. involve $\tau'$, $\tau''$, $\mathcal{H}$, $\mathcal{H}'$ and the perturbation variables (see Equations 66–67 of the report).

2.3.3. Initial conditions

The initial conditions deep in the tight coupling regime are given by ($f_\nu = \Omega_{\nu,0}/(\Omega_{\gamma,0}+\Omega_{\nu,0})$):

$$\Psi = -\frac{1}{\tfrac{2}{3}+\tfrac{2}{5}f_\nu},\quad \Phi = -\left(1+\frac{2f_\nu}{5}\right)\Psi,\quad \delta_{\rm CDM}=\delta_B=-\frac{3}{2}\Psi,\quad v_{\rm CDM}=v_B=-\frac{ck}{2\mathcal{H}}\Psi,$$
(77–80)
$$\Theta_0=-\frac{1}{2}\Psi,\quad \Theta_1=\frac{ck}{6\mathcal{H}}\Psi,\quad N_0=-\frac{1}{2}\Psi,\quad N_1=\frac{ck}{6\mathcal{H}}\Psi.$$
(81–82, 89–90)

2.4. CMB power-spectrum

Knowing the ionization history and the structure growth, the CMB power-spectrum can be computed. The CMB temperature field is expanded in spherical harmonics $T(\hat{n})=\sum_{\ell m} a_{\ell m}Y_{\ell m}(\hat{n})$, and the power spectrum is

$$C_\ell \equiv \langle|a_{\ell m}|^2\rangle = \langle a_{\ell m}a_{\ell m}^*\rangle.$$
(94)

Using the line-of-sight integration method [Seljak & Zaldarriaga (1996)], the temperature transfer functions are computed as

$$\Theta_\ell(k,x=0) = \int_{-\infty}^0 \tilde{S}(k,x)\,j_\ell\!\left[k(\eta_0-\eta)\right]dx,$$
(95)

where the source function $\tilde{S}$ is

$$\tilde{S}(k,x) = \tilde{g}\!\left[\Theta_0+\Psi+\frac{1}{4}\Pi\right]+e^{-\tau}(\Psi'-\Phi')-\frac{1}{ck}\frac{d}{dx}(\mathcal{H}\tilde{g}v_b)+\frac{3}{4c^2k^2}\frac{d^2}{dx^2}\!\left[\mathcal{H}^2\tilde{g}\Pi\right].$$
(96)

The four terms in $\tilde{S}$ correspond to: (1) the monopole (Sachs-Wolfe), (2) the Integrated Sachs-Wolfe effect (changing gravitational potentials), (3) a Doppler term, and (4) a polarization/quadrupole correction. The CMB power spectrum then reads

$$C_\ell = 4\pi\int_0^\infty A_s\!\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm pivot}}\right)^{n_s-1}\Theta_\ell^2(k)\,\frac{dk}{k}.$$
(99)

The matter power-spectrum is computed as

$$P(k,x) = |\Delta_M(k,x)|^2\,P_{\rm primordial}(k),\qquad \Delta_M(k,x)\equiv\frac{-3c^2k^2\Omega_{k,0}^{-1}\Phi(k,x)}{2a^2H_0^2\Omega_{M,0}}.$$
(100)

3. Implementation method

Four C++ classes have been implemented (all code is publicly available on GitHub): BackgroundCosmology, RecombinationHistory, Perturbations and PowerSpectrum. The perturbations are integrated by switching from the tight coupling system (only $\Theta_0$, $\Theta_1$) to the full system (up to $\ell_{\rm max}=8$ for the perturbation stage, $\ell_{\rm max}=1200$ for the power spectrum) when $\tau' < 10|\tau''|/|\tau'|$ or $|\tau'| < 10$. The line-of-sight integration (Equation 95) uses Bessel functions from the ComplexBessel library. All intermediate quantities are splined using cubic splines for fast evaluation.

The supernova fitting (Milestone I) uses a Metropolis MCMC with the chi-squared function

$$\chi^2(h,\Omega_{M,0},\Omega_{k,0}) = \sum_{i=1}^N\frac{\left[d_L(z_i,h,\Omega_{M,0},\Omega_{k,0})-d_L^{\rm obs}(z_i)\right]^2}{\sigma_i^2},$$
(104)

using the supernova data from Betoule et al. (2014) ($N=31$ data points).

4. Results

4.1. Solving the background cosmology of the Universe

The evolution of the relative energy densities of the Universe is presented in Figure 1. It can clearly be seen that there are three different regimes: radiation domination (early Universe), matter domination, and dark energy/$\Lambda$ domination (present era). From this data, the age of the Universe and the conformal time today can be computed: $t_{\rm Universe} = t(0) \approx 13.86$ Gyr and $\eta(0)/c \approx 46.52$ Gyr, respectively. 4.3. Solving the evolution of structure in the Universe

For all the main physical quantities in this subsection, four different scales (wave numbers) are presented: large scales ($k=0.001$ Mpc$^{-1}$ and $k=0.0001$ Mpc$^{-1}$), intermediate scales ($k=0.01$ Mpc$^{-1}$), and small scales ($k=0.1$ Mpc$^{-1}$).

4.4. Computing the CMB power-spectrum

The main result is shown in Figure 20. Reading the plot from large scales to small, we see the plateau on the largest scales, with the late ISW effect contributing to the increase on the very largest scales in $\ell\lesssim 10$. The first and third acoustic peaks are larger than the second and fourth, as the baryons with mass in a gravitational field enhance the compression modes (odd peaks) but not the rarefaction modes (even peaks). For higher number peaks, the exponential Silk damping suppression kicks in. The matter power spectrum (Figure 19) shows a turnover at the equality scale $k_{\rm eq}$.

5. Conclusions

Background cosmology of the Universe has been solved and each epoch has been determined, identifying in each of them the dominating substance. The age of the Universe has also been computed, being this $t_{\rm Universe} \approx 13.86$ Gyr.

Recombination history of the Universe has been solved, computing different important times during recombination, summarized in Table 3. The sound horizon at decoupling has also been computed, being this $r_s \approx 128.177$ Mpc.

The evolution of the structure in the Universe has been solved and the different kind of perturbations have been determined, showing that the metric in our Universe deviates from the Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLWR), implying that our Universe is not homogeneous nor isotropic.

The matter and CMB power-spectra have been computed with high accuracy, considering that neutrinos have been excluded, as well as Helium, reionization or photon polarization.
